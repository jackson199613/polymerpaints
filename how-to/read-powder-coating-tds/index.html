How to read a powder coating technical data sheet (TDS).

Read a TDS for system, use environment, cure, application data and the conditions behind each test result.

When you need this

You are comparing products, qualifying a new supplier or setting up your line. The TDS tells you how a powder is meant to be used, but its numbers only mean something together with the test conditions listed alongside them.

Related questions

  • Which powder coating system should I use for outdoor parts and which for indoor parts?
  • What does Qualicoat Class 1 mean, and which POLYMER products does it cover?
  • What documents can you provide with your powder coatings?

Steps

  1. Confirm system and use

    Check the resin system and whether the product is for indoor or outdoor use. This determines where it can go before any other figure matters.

  2. Note the cure schedule

    Read the cure window as metal temperature and time. Check that your oven can achieve it on your heaviest parts.

  3. Check powder properties

    Look at specific gravity and particle size. Specific gravity is needed for coverage estimates; particle size affects application and finish.

  4. Read the test conditions

    For mechanical and resistance results, note the substrate, pretreatment, film thickness and test method used. Results on laboratory panels may differ from your parts.

  5. Check storage guidance

    Note recommended storage conditions and shelf life, especially for low-cure grades.

  6. Cross-check certificates

    If an approval is mentioned, confirm that it covers that specific product, colour and finish.

1.4–1.8 g/cm³ depending on colour and gloss

Common mistakes

  • Comparing test results from two suppliers without checking film thickness and substrate.
  • Reading the cure temperature as oven air temperature.
  • Treating an in-house test result as an independent certificate.
  • Assuming an approval listed for one product applies to the whole range.

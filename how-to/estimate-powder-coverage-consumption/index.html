How to estimate powder coating coverage and consumption.

Calculate theoretical coverage from specific gravity and film thickness, then adjust for your real utilisation.

When you need this

You are comparing products, quoting a job or planning stock. Kilogram price alone is misleading, because powders with different specific gravities cover different areas at the same film thickness.

Related questions

  • What is the minimum order quantity for POLYMER powder coatings?

Steps

  1. Get specific gravity

    Take the specific gravity from the product's TDS. Polyester powders in our catalogue range from 1.4 to 1.8 g/cm³ depending on colour and gloss.

  2. Set film thickness

    Use your actual average cured film thickness in µm, not the minimum.

  3. Calculate theoretical coverage

    Theoretical coverage (m²/kg) = 1000 ÷ (specific gravity × film thickness in µm). For example, specific gravity 1.5 at 60 µm gives about 11.1 m²/kg.

  4. Apply utilisation

    Multiply by your overall utilisation, which reflects first-pass transfer efficiency, recovery and losses. A line with good recovery uses far more of the powder than one without.

  5. Calculate consumption

    Divide the area to be coated by the practical coverage to estimate kilograms needed, then add an allowance for colour changes.

Parameters

Formula
m²/kg = 1000 / (SG × µm)
Specific gravity
per TDS (polyester catalogue 1.4–1.8 g/cm³)
Film thickness
Your measured average
Utilisation
Line-specific; measure on your line

Common mistakes

  • Comparing powders by price per kilogram without considering specific gravity.
  • Using the minimum specified thickness instead of the real average.
  • Ignoring overspray that is not recovered.
  • Treating the example figure as a product specification.

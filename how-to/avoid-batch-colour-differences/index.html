How to avoid colour differences between powder coating batches.

Fix one approved reference, keep application and cure stable, and avoid mixing batches on the same job.

When you need this

Parts coated weeks apart do not match, or assemblies combine parts from different deliveries. Batch differences can come from the powder, but often come from changes in film thickness, cure or how colour is judged.

Related questions

  • What is your lead time for powder coating orders?
  • Can you match a RAL colour or a colour from my own sample?
  • How do you make sure repeat orders match the colour I approved?

Steps

  1. Fix one reference

    Approve a panel and keep it as the master. POLYMER records it as a version and produces repeat orders to it.

  2. Order by version

    Quote the colour and approved version on every order so the right reference is used.

  3. Keep film thickness steady

    Hold a consistent target thickness; colour, gloss and textures shift when thickness changes.

  4. Keep cure consistent

    Control metal temperature and time, as overbaking can shift colour, especially on whites.

  5. Judge colour the same way

    Use the same light source or instrument and the same tolerance every time.

  6. Avoid mixing batches

    Finish one batch before opening the next on a single job or assembly, and record batch numbers on coated parts.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing new parts with old parts that have aged in use instead of with the master panel.
  • Mixing two batches in the hopper during one job.
  • Changing gun settings or line speed without checking thickness.
  • Judging colour under different lighting each time.

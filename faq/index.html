Products & systems

Which powder coating system should I use for outdoor parts and which for indoor parts?

Use polyester for outdoor and UV-exposed parts. Use epoxy-polyester hybrid for indoor decorative parts and epoxy for indoor functional parts that need chemical resistance or hardness.

POLYMER supplies three thermosetting systems, and the choice is driven mainly by where the coated part will be used. Polyester is the weather-resistant system in our catalogue and is the one we recommend for architectural aluminium, outdoor furniture and lighting, air-conditioner housings, outdoor cabinets and agricultural machinery.

The epoxy-polyester hybrid gives good flow and gloss and is intended for indoor items such as appliances, steel furniture, shelving and indoor enclosures. Epoxy offers good chemical resistance, hardness and adhesion, and suits indoor functional parts such as safes, security doors, instrument housings and fitness equipment.

Neither epoxy nor hybrid should be specified for outdoor use. If one product range contains both indoor and outdoor parts, select the system part by part rather than using one powder for everything. Final suitability is confirmed on your parts, with your pretreatment and cure conditions.

Can epoxy-polyester hybrid powder be used outdoors?

No. Our hybrid powders are intended for indoor use and we do not specify them for outdoor or UV-exposed parts; use polyester instead.

Epoxy-polyester hybrids combine good flow, high gloss and good mechanical properties, and their heat and light stability is better than that of pure epoxy. That makes them a strong choice for appliances, steel furniture and indoor cabinets, but it does not make them an outdoor product.

The epoxy part of the binder is sensitive to UV light. Outdoors, hybrid coatings can lose gloss, chalk and change colour much sooner than a polyester designed for weathering. For that reason POLYMER does not recommend hybrid for exterior parts, even when the part looks well protected or is only partly exposed.

If a part may see sunlight, for example an outdoor cabinet, an air-conditioner housing or garden furniture, please tell us so we can propose a polyester powder in the colour and finish you need.

When should I consider a low-cure powder coating?

Consider low-cure grades when your oven cannot reach or hold the standard cure, when heavy parts are slow to heat, or when you want to reduce oven energy or increase line speed.

All three POLYMER systems are available in standard-cure and low-cure grades. A low-cure grade reaches full cure at a lower metal temperature or in a shorter time than the standard grade; the actual schedule for each product is given on its TDS.

Typical reasons to consider low cure are: an oven that struggles to bring thick sections or mixed-mass loads up to temperature, a wish to reduce gas or electricity use, a need to run the line faster, and parts or assemblies that should not be exposed to higher temperatures.

Low-cure powders are more reactive, so storage temperature and stock rotation matter more. Before switching, measure your real metal temperature profile with an oven data logger and run a line trial, then confirm cure and appearance on representative parts.

Why is epoxy powder for indoor use only, and why can it turn yellow?

Epoxy binders break down under UV light, so they chalk and discolour outdoors. They can also yellow if baked too hot or for too long.

Epoxy powders give strong adhesion, hardness and chemical resistance, which is why they are used for indoor functional parts. Their weakness is UV light: outdoors, the surface chalks and loses colour and gloss relatively quickly. POLYMER epoxy powders are therefore for indoor use only and should not be used directly outdoors.

Epoxy can also yellow in the oven. When the metal temperature is too high or the part stays in the oven too long, the film can turn yellow, which is most visible on whites and pale colours. Check that your oven profile matches the cure window on the product TDS, especially on lines with mixed part thicknesses.

Our epoxy powders are decorative and protective coatings for indoor parts. They are not fusion-bonded epoxy (FBE/3PE) pipeline coatings or heavy-duty anticorrosion systems.

Is your epoxy powder suitable for pipelines or heavy-duty anti-corrosion?

No. Our epoxy is a decorative and functional powder for indoor metal parts. We do not make FBE or 3PE pipeline coatings or heavy-duty anti-corrosion systems.

Pure epoxy gives very good adhesion, chemical resistance and electrical insulation indoors, but it chalks and yellows under UV, so it is not used for direct outdoor exposure.

If your part needs a specified anti-corrosion system, tell us the standard and environment and we will say honestly whether we can support it.

Colour, gloss & effects

Can you match a RAL colour or a colour from my own sample?

Yes. We match RAL colours and customer-specified colours from physical samples, in all three powder systems.

For RAL colours, the code is a starting point, but gloss, texture and film thickness all change how a colour looks. We therefore recommend sending a physical reference as well, such as a coated part, a panel or the powder you currently use.

For a custom colour, a physical sample is essential. Please tell us the system or environment (indoor or outdoor), target gloss band, finish or effect, substrate, typical film thickness and your cure conditions, and how you judge colour (light source, instrument or visual check).

We match the colour, prepare a panel and send it for your approval. Once you approve it, the panel is recorded as a version so later orders are produced to the same reference. Metallic and textured colours are always confirmed on physical panels, because they cannot be judged from a code alone.

Basis: POLYMER colour-matching process; general colour-matching practice. · Colours & finishes →

How do you make sure repeat orders match the colour I approved?

Each approved panel is recorded as a version with its system, colour, gloss, texture, film thickness and cure, and repeat orders are produced and checked against that version.

A colour is only meaningful together with the conditions it was made under. When you approve a panel, POLYMER records it as a version that links the powder system, colour, gloss, texture, film thickness and cure conditions.

Samples, trial orders and later bulk orders all refer to the same version. In production, each batch is checked in-house for colour, gloss and the other agreed properties before release, using the approved version as the reference.

On your side, keep your retained approved panel and apply the powder at similar film thickness and cure conditions; large changes in either can shift colour or gloss even when the powder is identical. If you want to change gloss, texture or system, we create a new version rather than modifying the old one, so the history stays clear.

Basis: POLYMER panel version management; in-house batch checks. · Quality & testing →

What gloss levels and surface effects do you offer?

Gloss bands are high gloss (≥90), satin/matt (11–80) and dead matt (≤10). Effects include smooth, sand, wrinkle, vein, fine texture, hammer tone, pattern, bonded metallic, transparent/translucent and fluorescent.

All three systems are available in a range of gloss levels: high gloss at 90 or above, satin and matt between 11 and 80, and dead matt at 10 or below. The exact target within a band is set to your requirement.

Surface effects include smooth, sand texture, wrinkle, vein (net) texture, fine texture, hammer tone, pattern finishes such as antique and art effects, bonded metallics, transparent and translucent powders, and fluorescent colours.

Not every combination exists in every system, so we confirm the system, colour and effect together. Textures and metallics are judged on physical panels and, ideally, on your parts with your spray equipment, because film thickness and application settings affect how they look. Tell us the end use and we will suggest suitable combinations.

What is bonded metallic powder and why does it matter?

In bonded metallic powder the metallic pigment is attached to the powder particles, which reduces separation during spraying and recovery and helps keep the effect consistent.

Metallic and pearlescent effects come from flake pigments. If the flakes are simply dry-blended with the base powder, they can charge and travel differently from the powder particles. The result can be uneven appearance, differences between virgin and recovered powder, and drift in the effect as a job runs.

Bonding attaches the flakes to the powder particles under controlled heat and mixing, so they move through the gun and recovery system together. POLYMER has two metallic bonding machines for this.

Bonding reduces separation, but metallic effects still depend on application. Viewing angle, film thickness, gun settings and the ratio of recovered powder all influence the final look. We therefore confirm each metallic effect on physical panels and under your spraying conditions before bulk supply.

What is the minimum order quantity for POLYMER powder coatings?

The minimum order quantity is 1,000 kg per order.

POLYMER works with importers and distributors, metal product manufacturers and coating applicators that have regular volume, so our MOQ is 1,000 kg per order. We do not accept one-off orders below that level.

When you send an enquiry, it helps to tell us the expected quantity, how it is split across colours and finishes, and how often you expect to reorder. This lets us plan production and confirm the order arrangement and lead time.

If you are a distributor, an initial order is typically built from a mix of standard colours and the effects your customers ask for most; we can discuss that mix with you before you order.

What is your lead time for powder coating orders?

Standard lead time is within 10 days. Lead time for complex custom colours and large volumes is confirmed with each order.

For repeat orders of an approved colour, production is made to the recorded panel version, which avoids re-matching and helps keep the schedule predictable. Telling us your reorder pattern in advance makes planning easier on both sides.

How long can powder be stored, and how is it shipped?

Store powder cool, dry and ventilated. Storage stability is tested at 6 months below 30 °C; the SDS asks for storage below 35 °C. The powder is not classified as dangerous goods for transport.

Keep cartons closed and away from heat and direct sun, and let cold powder reach room temperature before opening. Low-cure grades are more sensitive to heat, so rotate stock first in, first out.

Packing format is confirmed with the quotation.

Certificates & quality

What does Qualicoat Class 1 mean, and which POLYMER products does it cover?

Qualicoat Class 1 is the standard-durability approval level for powder coatings on architectural aluminium. At POLYMER it covers one polyester system, PLM100611, under licence P-2199.

Qualicoat is a quality label for coatings on aluminium used in architecture. Its Class 1 level is the standard-durability category. Higher durability classes exist, and POLYMER does not hold them.

The approval applies to our polyester system PLM100611, licence P-2199, gloss category 3. It does not include structured-texture or metallic extensions, and it does not extend to our other polyester products, to epoxy or hybrid powders, or to every colour we make. The current certificate is valid until 31 December 2026 and will be updated when renewed.

If your project requires a Qualicoat-approved powder, tell us the colour, gloss and finish so we can confirm whether it falls within the approved system. If a specification calls for a higher Qualicoat class, that is outside our current approval.

Basis: POLYMER Qualicoat certificate (licence P-2199) as stated in company records. · Quality & certificates →

What documents can you provide with your powder coatings?

On request we supply the product catalogue, technical and safety data sheets (TDS and SDS), ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 certificates, and the Qualicoat Class 1 certificate for polyester system PLM100611.

A product's TDS lists the product's properties, cure conditions and storage guidance, and its SDS covers safe handling and transport. Our catalogue describes the three systems, cure grades, gloss bands and effects. Colour cards and panels are available for evaluation.

Our ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 certificates cover our quality and environmental management systems. They are not product certifications. The Qualicoat Class 1 certificate (licence P-2199) applies only to polyester system PLM100611.

Test results we share come from our in-house testing and are not third-party reports. If your project needs an independent report or a specific certification, tell us early so we can confirm what is possible. We do not currently offer UL, IATF 16949 or food-contact approved products.

Basis: POLYMER certificates and document set. · Downloads →

What testing do you carry out in-house, and what are its limits?

Our in-house tests cover colour, 60° gloss, film thickness, particle size, salt spray, UV accelerated ageing, pencil hardness, impact, 180° bend, cross-cut adhesion and storage stability. These are internal tests; separately, an SGS report confirms EU RoHS compliance for a tested black powder sample.

We use these tests to develop and approve panels, check batches before release and support technical discussions with customers. Colour, gloss and particle size are used to keep batches consistent with the approved version; mechanical tests check that the film is flexible and adherent; salt spray and UV accelerated ageing are used to compare products under laboratory conditions.

These results have limits. They are produced in-house, not by an accredited third party. Accelerated tests indicate relative performance; they do not predict an outdoor lifetime in years. Results also depend on the substrate, pretreatment, film thickness and cure used, which may differ from your line.

For a project, we recommend testing on your own parts with your process. If an independent report is required, tell us at the enquiry stage.

Basis: POLYMER in-house test capability; general testing practice. · Quality & testing →

An SGS report (XMNEC26001081101, 12 May 2026) found a black powder sample compliant with EU RoHS (EU) 2015/863, with all ten restricted substances not detected.

What is your quality control process?

Every batch passes two checkpoints before packing: a production self-check after sieving and a laboratory test against the approved panel.

The laboratory checks colour with a colorimeter and light box, 60° gloss, film thickness, particle size, appearance, pencil hardness, impact, 180° bend and cross-cut adhesion. A batch that fails goes back to the formula and premix stage.

We do not hold Qualicoat Class 2 or Class 3, UL, IATF 16949 or food-contact approvals.

We state this plainly so your approval file stays clean. If a project needs one of these, tell us early and we will say whether we can help.

Samples & trials

How do I request a sample, and what should I send you?

Send us your part details, environment, target colour and finish, process conditions and expected volume, plus a physical colour reference where possible. We then propose a product and prepare a panel or sample.

A good sample starts with a clear brief. Please send: your company and contact; the part name, use, substrate and a photo; whether it is used indoors or outdoors and any humidity, coastal or chemical exposure; the colour code or a physical sample, gloss and effect; pretreatment, target film thickness, spray equipment and actual cure conditions; any standards or approvals the coating must meet; and your expected volume and colour structure.

If you are replacing an existing powder, its product name, technical data sheet and the problem you want to solve are also useful.

We use this to select the system, match the colour and prepare a panel or sample powder. After you test it on your parts, we agree any adjustments and record the approved version for production.

Basis: POLYMER sample and order process. · Request a sample →

What do I receive when I request a sample?

Panels sprayed in the matched colour and finish, or sample powder for a trial on your line, depending on what you need to evaluate.

Each panel is recorded with its system, colour, gloss, texture, film thickness and cure, so the version you approve can be reproduced. Product documents are sent with the sample on request.

Basis: POLYMER sample process. · Request a sample →

Why should I run a line trial before ordering?

Because your pretreatment, spray equipment, film thickness and oven decide the final result as much as the powder does.

Spray the sample on real parts, measure the actual metal temperature with an oven data logger and check appearance, adhesion and impact against your own acceptance criteria.
